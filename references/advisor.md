# Advisor Protocol

Read only in Advisor mode. Diagnose the most actionable weakness and prescribe
the smallest useful intervention; do not inventory the course.

If the learner asks to attempt, correct, confirm, and transfer interactively,
route the subsequent turns to `session-protocol.md`; an Advisor plan must not
simulate those future responses or persist a profile.

## Evidence and diagnosis

Record only what the learner supplied: course, deadline, study time, attempted
and correct counts, completion time, written work/error log, and confidence.
Keep missing values unknown. Do not assume access to a scoring guide, question
bank, teacher resource, or other unmentioned material. Ask only when the answer
would materially change the intervention; otherwise disclose the proxy.

An aggregate Unit score establishes neither a primary Topic nor a Practice,
first error, or misconception. A specific quiz label may establish its content
Topic, but its score still establishes no causal misconception. In either case,
collect one supplied missed prompt with the learner's complete work, or give one
brief discriminating probe, before prescribing a targeted drill. For an
aggregate Unit score with no item-level evidence, do not select even a
"screening" Topic or launch a multi-item diagnostic, drill, calendar, or retest:
ask for exactly one missed prompt plus complete work, or give exactly one brief
probe, then wait. Do not ask the learner to redo or inspect earlier questions
that were not supplied. When relative weaknesses cannot be ranked, say they are
unknown and name the exact proxies used for any provisional ordering.

Classify evidenced problems as one or more of:

- conceptual: a governing definition, relation, or condition is misapplied;
- modeling: quantities, constraints, or their equation are wrong;
- procedure/computation: setup is sound but execution fails;
- representation: information is lost across verbal, graphical, numerical,
  tabular, or analytical forms;
- justification: an argument, condition, or supporting work is missing;
- reading: a stated condition, quantity, unit, or instruction is missed;
- pacing: a viable method is too slow after other causes are ruled out.

Elapsed time is only an observation until it is compared with a caller-supplied
target or a comparable learner baseline. Never invent a percentage improvement,
time limit, or pacing exit threshold. To locate a repeatable source of time use,
collect three comparable unseen attempts and record cumulative times after
reading/method selection, unsimplified setup, algebra/simplification, and
checking. Treat a phase as the next target only if it is the largest on at least
two of three attempts, and only after all three attempts are independently
correct with a valid method setup; otherwise collect more evidence. Preserve
correctness while timing.

Separate observed symptoms from hypothesized causes. Infer causes only from
work or an error log. Accuracy, time, and self-report are provisional ranking
proxies. Never treat catalog order as a dependency graph.

## Minimum intervention and exit

Rank by evidence strength, impact, transfer leverage, deadline, and available
time. Preserve demonstrated strengths with light maintenance. Select one to
three tasks unless the learner asks for broader coverage.

Each task includes:

- **Why now:** evidence, cause hypothesis, uncertainty, and priority reason;
- **Practice:** a concrete method and bounded amount;
- **Exit standard:** measurable accuracy, setup, justification, independence,
  or time threshold.

Map and validate the relevant content Topic; the mapping is metadata, not the
priority reason. Retest with an unseen problem that changes context, values, or
representation. State the passing result. Until it occurs, call the
intervention provisional and do not claim mastery.

When the user asks for the next item or asks you to choose the next step and the
item-level evidence supports a confirmation or transfer, provide exactly one
complete unseen prompt in the response, not an example category or description
of what a future item should contain. Keep that item hidden: do not include its
answer, completed setup, success value, or an answer-producing formula hint. If
the evidence repeatedly shows an omitted inner derivative, first use two short
outer/inner labeling drills, then use at least two structurally different,
unseen, independent no-hint derivatives before passing the intervention. More
generally, when the same rule error appears across multiple structurally
different attempts, one correct unseen item is not enough.

Do not reveal the answer, completed setup, or success value for a task described
as unseen, independent, confirmation, transfer, or an exit check in the same
response. State only its observable success conditions and wait for the real
attempt.

Give only the requested horizon; do not create a calendar unless requested.
