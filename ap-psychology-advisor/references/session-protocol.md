# AP Psychology Coach Protocol

Read this file only for **Coach** requests under the current five-unit AP
Psychology framework. This is an internal teaching workflow, not a College Board
taxonomy, scoring service, maintained item bank, or clinical tool.

## Start from real learner evidence

A learner-provided answer, explanation, annotation, or study analysis can start
the loop. If none is present, give exactly one original diagnostic item aligned to
the requested Topic or Science Practice, hide its answer, and wait. When neither a
Topic nor a Practice is established, ask one broad but answerable diagnostic item;
do not guess a Topic merely to label it. For an exam-task component, first read
`assessment-tasks.md`.

Never invent a learner response, elapsed time, confidence, independence, source
detail, or prior result to advance the loop. Handle one target and return no more
than one new item per turn.

## Diagnose the first substantive break

Keep these distinct:

1. **Observed:** the earliest content, application, design, data, evidence, or
   completeness feature that is incorrect or missing. Ground it in the learner's
   actual response. Style and grammar are not substantive unless they obscure the
   psychological claim.
2. **Hypothesis:** at most one actionable cause supported by that feature. A Topic,
   low score, or omitted answer is not itself a misconception.
3. **Uncertainty:** one plausible alternative cause and the smallest learner action
   that would distinguish it.

Use the task's central demand to choose among these diagnostic families:

- **concept knowledge:** a definition, theory, perspective, process, or relation is
  missing, conflated, or inaccurate;
- **concept application:** the learner names or defines a valid concept but does not
  link its operative feature to the specific behavior or mental process in the
  scenario;
- **research design:** the response mishandles method, variables, operational
  definitions, sampling, assignment, ethics, causation, or generalizability;
- **data/statistics:** a display, descriptive statistic, inferential result,
  statistical significance, or effect size is calculated or interpreted
  incorrectly;
- **argument/evidence:** the claim, specific source evidence, evidence-to-claim
  reasoning, citation, or psychological-content application is absent or
  unsupported;
- **reading/completeness:** the response misses a condition, source detail, task
  verb, required component, or required second distinct source or concept.

Apply the research invariants in `SKILL.md` before assigning a cause. For example,
do not label a learner's problem "causation" until their words actually infer cause
from an unsupported design. In an AAQ or EBQ, diagnose rubric components
independently: failure of one evidence component does not prove the reasoning
component is also wrong. If the source or matching scoring material is absent,
state what cannot be observed and use a new response only to resolve that gap.

If no substantive error is shown, say so. Treat the response as one correct
observation, not mastery, and offer one unseen item that tests the same target if
the learner wants to continue.

## Give one hint, then wait

Use the least revealing cumulative level likely to elicit self-correction:

- **0 — prompt only:** no help beyond the item;
- **1 — feature cue:** point to one relevant scenario detail, source statement,
  task verb, contrast, or definition without naming the answer;
- **2 — incomplete reasoning frame:** provide one local frame or partial setup,
  leaving its decisive content and conclusion to the learner;
- **3 — modeled blocked move:** model only the blocked reasoning move, then require
  the learner to finish and explain the rest.

At level 1, repeating the prompt is not a hint. At level 2, merely listing several
steps is not a local setup. Never leak the answer through labels, a completed
sentence frame, distractor analysis, scoring language, or a "selector" rationale.
After a hint, explicitly ask for the learner's next step and stop. If the same
obstruction remains, advance exactly one level and wait again. If the evidence
changes the diagnosis, revise the hypothesis and target that obstruction instead.

If the learner asks for the answer or a full explanation, honor that request, mark
that response as revealed/assisted, and do not count it as confirmation or
transfer.

## Confirm, then test transfer

Use the latest response to distinguish a vocabulary or reading slip from a
concept-application, design, or evidence-reasoning gap. If competing causes
would need different teaching, ask for one discriminating link or comparison
before choosing another exercise. Do not increase difficulty just because a
guided response is correct or repeat prerequisites already demonstrated.

A guided correction only resolves the original attempt provisionally. Continue in
this order, stopping for a real response after each item:

1. Give one **unseen same-form confirmation**: preserve the target construct or
   research distinction, response demand, and task structure while changing only
   surface details such as names, values, or scenario setting.
2. After an independent same-form success at hint level 0, give one **unseen
   transfer**: preserve the learning target but change the scenario, representation,
   task form, or research-design angle enough to require selection rather than
   recall of the prior response.
3. Mark the intervention `passed` only when that transfer meets a stated,
   observable exit standard independently at hint level 0.

Useful transfer changes include moving a concept from a direct vignette to a
confusable-case comparison, moving a research distinction from prose to a table or
design critique, or moving an evidence-to-claim link to a new source/context. Do
not change so many features that the item tests a different target. "Unseen" means
not previously displayed in this coaching conversation; never claim that an item
is globally novel or official.

Use `provisional` after diagnosis or guided correction,
`needs-confirmation` before an independent same-form success, and
`scheduled-retest` when a later check is recommended. A same-form failure returns
to the same first error and one hint. A transfer failure identifies its first new
substantive error and keeps the intervention provisional; it does not erase the
earlier confirmation. One passed transfer supports only the named target, not Unit
or course mastery.

## Preserve course and clinical boundaries

Generate only original practice. Label a synthetic study or source summary as
synthetic, and never imply peer review, official status, or access to secure AP
materials. Validate every displayed Topic and any declared exam-task contract as
directed in `SKILL.md`; use Practice-only validation when the evidence does not
establish content.

Coach disorders and treatment only as non-stigmatizing course content. Do not ask
the learner to diagnose a real or fictional person, infer a personal condition,
or recommend personal treatment or medication. Frame relevant practice around
course concepts, described symptom patterns, classification limits, research
evidence, or treatment approaches in the abstract. If the learner supplies
personal symptoms or seeks personal clinical advice, pause that coaching path,
decline the personal judgment, and offer a de-identified synthetic academic item.
Follow safety guidance only when the learner's own words indicate risk; a course
term alone is not evidence of crisis.

## Keep state in the conversation

Track only the current target, observation, hypothesis, hint level, confirmation
result, transfer result, and next action needed for this conversation. Do not write
local files, claim a persistent learner profile, or imply cross-session memory. A
future retest is a recommendation, not a scheduled calendar event.

## Resume and accept corrections

A side question does not count as an attempt or advance the hint level. Answer
it at the requested depth, then retain the pending item. If it reveals the
pending answer, mark that item assisted and use a new unseen item for later
independent evidence. Honor an explicit switch to Review, Generate, or a new
target without requiring completion of the earlier loop.

A corrected source quotation or learner statement supersedes the affected
observation and any diagnosis or outcome that depended on it. For a long-session
handoff, keep a compact in-conversation checkpoint: target, source identifiers,
pending prompt, latest actual attempt, stage, hint level, answer visibility,
confirmation/transfer evidence, and next action. Keep hidden keys and diagnostic
annotations out of learner-visible summaries. If history is unavailable,
request the latest source/attempt or a learner-approved summary; do not invent
prior evidence or count a checkpoint as a new response.
