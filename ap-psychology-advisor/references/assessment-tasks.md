# Current AP Psychology Assessment Tasks

Read for exam-oriented generation or review. These constraints reflect the Fall
2025 CED plus the October 2025 clarifications, checked 2026-08-28 for the 2026–27
school year. Recheck official College Board sources before stating changeable exam
facts. For current task wording and pacing behavior, the released 2026 FRQ is the
most recent public example recorded in the boundary source metadata.

## Multiple-choice

- Current section: 75 questions in 90 minutes, 66.7% of the exam score.
- Includes discrete and set-based items.
- Practices 1–3 are assessed; Practice 4 is FRQ-only. Current MCQ practice
  weighting is approximately 65% Practice 1, 25% Practice 2, and 10% Practice 3.
- Each content Unit represents 15%–25% of the MCQ section, not of the whole exam.

An original item needs one best answer, plausible distractors based on distinct
reasoning errors, and enough stimulus information to answer without hidden facts.
Do not claim a distractor is common or official without evidence.

## Article Analysis Question (AAQ)

A current full AAQ uses one summarized peer-reviewed source and assesses Practices
2, 3, and 4. Score its six parts independently for 7 points:

- **A — research method (0–1):** accurately identify the method used.
- **B — operational definition (0–1):** state the measurable or quantifiable
  definition of the requested variable as used in the study.
- **C — statistic interpretation (0–1):** accurately interpret the specified
  statistic in the study context; a definition or numerical restatement alone is
  insufficient.
- **D — ethical guideline and application (0–1):** identify a guideline actually
  described in the source **and** describe how the researchers applied it. Merely
  naming a guideline, or proposing one not described, earns 0.
- **E — generalizability (0–1):** make a claim about a target population and
  support it with specific participant or sample characteristics. Sample size,
  reliability, or validity alone does not establish generalizability.
- **F — argumentation (0–2):** award 0 when there is no accurate support/refute
  explanation; 1 when the response uses a study result without explaining the
  relationship, or explains the relationship without a study result; 2 when it
  accurately uses a specific result to explain how it supports or refutes the
  target concept or hypothesis.

Do not infer an operational definition, sampling method, population detail, or
result absent from the summary. When generating a full AAQ, describe several
identifiable ethical guidelines in the synthetic source summary so Part D does not
have only one engineered answer.

## Evidence-Based Question (EBQ)

A current full EBQ uses three summarized peer-reviewed sources on one topic and
assesses Practices 1 and 4. Score its five elements independently for 7 points:

- **A — claim (0–1):** propose a specific, defensible claim responsive to the
  question.
- **B(i) — evidence (0–1):** use accurate, specific, relevant evidence and
  correctly cite its source.
- **B(ii) — explanation and application (0–2):** award 0 when the response does
  not accurately explain the evidence-to-claim relationship; 1 for an accurate
  relationship without an applied psychological perspective, theory, concept, or
  research finding; 2 when accurate reasoning also correctly applies that AP
  Psychology content.
- **C(i) — second evidence (0–1):** meet the B(i) criteria with different evidence
  from a source different from the one used for B(i).
- **C(ii) — second explanation and application (0–2):** apply the B(ii) scale; the
  2-point application must use a different psychological perspective, theory,
  concept, or research finding from B(ii).

Source paraphrase alone is not reasoning. Merely naming a psychological concept is
not application. A citation error, nonspecific evidence, or same-source error can
lose B(i) or C(i) without automatically zeroing B(ii) or C(ii); judge the
evidence-to-claim explanation and content application independently under their own
decision rules.

## Source and scoring integrity

- Do not invent a study's peer-reviewed status, authors, sample, method, data, or
  findings. Label synthetic practice stimuli and do not call them official or
  peer-reviewed.
- Use a verified source or user-provided source summary for source-specific
  scoring. If it is missing, state what cannot be judged.
- Generate original tasks. Do not reconstruct secure AP Classroom, Progress Check,
  or Practice Exam questions.
- Give a numeric score for a released question only when both the question and the
  official scoring guide match its year, set, and question. Otherwise give
  explicitly unscored conceptual feedback and name the parts that cannot be
  judged.
- Use the matching 2025 scoring guide for 2025 work; do not silently apply the
  October 2025 clarification. As checked 2026-08-28, AP Central lists the 2026
  released questions but no 2026 scoring guide, samples, or commentary, so 2026
  work receives clearly labeled provisional, unscored feedback until those are
  public.
- An original current-framework task may receive a `current-rubric estimate` only
  when it is labeled original or synthetic. Never call it an official question or
  official score.
- The current exam is fully digital and the FRQ section totals 70 minutes. The
  25-minute AAQ and 45-minute EBQ split, including suggested reading time, is
  guidance rather than a locked per-question timer.

## Legacy warning

Current content uses five Units and AAQ/EBQ. Treat nine Units, 100 MCQs in 70
minutes, 50 minutes for two FRQs, or FRQs named only Concept Application and
Research Design as pre-2025 legacy markers. Never translate an old Topic code
silently or apply the current AAQ/EBQ rubric to 2024-or-earlier tasks. A
year/task-rubric mismatch bars a numeric score; older released questions remain
usable only for clearly bounded, unscored conceptual practice unless their exact
contemporaneous scoring materials are present.
