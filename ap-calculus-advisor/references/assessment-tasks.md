# AP Precalculus and Calculus Assessment-Task Contract

Read this reference before generating or reviewing an `exam-oriented` AP
Precalculus or Calculus task. The current administration facts below were
checked on 2026-09-03 for the May 2027 exams. Recheck the linked College Board
pages before stating changeable format, timing, weighting, calculator,
delivery, or policy facts.

## Scope and source status

- AP Precalculus exam-oriented coverage is Units 1–3 only. Unit 4 remains
  instructional and is not assessed on the AP Exam. Adaptive Coach coverage
  includes Units 1–4, with two maintained diagnostic patterns per Unit.
- AP Calculus AB adaptive Coach coverage is Units 1–8, excluding BC-only
  Topics embedded in those Units: 6.11–6.13, 7.5, 7.9, and 8.13. Topic 6.14 is
  shared only when the selected technique itself is AB content.
- AP Calculus BC adaptive Coach coverage is Units 1–10. It reuses maintained AB
  items for shared content and adds selected BC-only patterns in Units 6–10;
  the bank is not an exhaustive Topic inventory.
- The current Calculus framework is the Fall 2020 CED in its consolidated 2026
  PDF, with the Fall 2026 clarifications. It is not a new Fall 2026 framework.
- The Calculus course page and framework table use “Differentiation: Definition
  and Fundamental Properties” for Unit 2 and “Analytical Applications of
  Differentiation” for Unit 5. The current CED exam-information table contains
  alternate titles. Use the course-page/framework-table titles as canonical
  output and preserve the aliases only as source metadata.

## Current May 2027 AP Precalculus task contract

The exam lasts 2 hours 55 minutes and is hybrid digital: students answer
multiple-choice questions and view free-response questions in Bluebook, then
handwrite free-response work in a paper booklet.

### Multiple choice

- 42 questions, 105 minutes, 62.5% of the exam score.
- Part A: 29 questions, 65 minutes, calculator not permitted, 43.75% of the
  exam score.
- Part B: 13 questions, 40 minutes, graphing-calculator-required section,
  18.75% of the exam score.
- Units 1–3 and Mathematical Practices 1–3 may be assessed.
- Within the multiple-choice section, the published Unit ranges are Unit 1
  30–40%, Unit 2 25–40%, and Unit 3 30–35%. Across the whole exam, the
  published Practice ranges are Practice 1 35–50%, Practice 2 20–30%, and
  Practice 3 30–40%.
- One best answer; no written justification is scored.

`calculator-required section` is a part-level condition. It means students
must have a graphing calculator available, not that every Part B item
inherently requires its use.

### Free response

- 4 questions, 70 minutes, 37.5% of the exam score; each question is worth 6
  points.
- Part A: Questions 1–2, 35 minutes, graphing-calculator-required section,
  18.75% of the exam score.
- Part B: Questions 3–4, 35 minutes, calculator not permitted, 18.75% of the
  exam score.

| `free_response_type` | Official task model | Part | Units | Context | Mathematical Practices |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `function-concepts` | Function Concepts | II-A | 1–2 | No real-world context | 1, 2, 3 |
| `modeling-non-periodic-context` | Modeling a Non-Periodic Context | II-A | 1–2 | Real-world | 1, 3 |
| `modeling-periodic-context` | Modeling a Periodic Context | II-B | 3 | Real-world | 1, 2, 3 |
| `symbolic-manipulations` | Symbolic Manipulations | II-B | 2–3 | No real-world context | 1 |

A full Function Concepts task must use analytical, graphical, and numerical
representations. The official Precalculus representation families are
analytical, graphical, numerical, and verbal. The shared machine contract
retains `tabular` only as an internal subtype of numerical representation; it
is not a fifth official family. Do not invent stricter representation bundles
for the other three task models when the official task description does not
establish them.

All free-response tasks require students to show supporting work. Keep that
rule separate from logical justification: a subpart requires a written
rationale when its verb demands one. A full Function Concepts or Modeling a
Non-Periodic Context task includes the fixed Communication and Reasoning
rationale component and therefore records `justification: required`; partial
tasks remain verb-dependent.

For Precalculus free response, record `question_type: free-response` and one
of the four `free_response_type` values. The subtype is forbidden for
multiple-choice and AP Calculus tasks.

## Current May 2027 AP Calculus task contract

The exam is hybrid digital: students answer multiple-choice questions and view
free-response questions in Bluebook, then handwrite free-response work in a
paper booklet.

### Multiple choice

- 42 questions, 100 minutes, 50% of the exam score.
- Part A: 29 questions, 62 minutes, calculator not permitted.
- Part B: 13 questions, 38 minutes, calculator-required section.
- One best answer; no written justification is scored.
- Practices 1–3 may be assessed. Practice 4 is not assessed in this section.
- Representations may be analytical, graphical, tabular, numerical, or verbal.

`calculator-required section` is a part-level condition. It does not claim that
every Part B item inherently needs calculator use.

### Free response

- 6 questions, 90 minutes, 50% of the exam score.
- Part A: 2 questions, 30 minutes, calculator-required section.
- Part B: 4 questions, 60 minutes, calculator not permitted.
- All four Mathematical Practices may be assessed.
- A prompt that asks students to explain, justify, or verify must require the
  relevant reasoning and conditions. An identify/indicate prompt does not
  require elaboration unless it explicitly asks for it.
- Calculator-derived results must show the mathematical setup and the result.
  Other built-in features or programs require the mathematical steps that
  produce the result.

For generated tasks, record question type, calculator condition,
representation, and justification requirement separately. Also record the
named free-response type when the Precalculus contract requires it. A Topic
mapping alone never establishes an exam task.

## Originality, scoring, and visibility

- Generate original tasks. Do not reproduce AP Classroom, Progress Check,
  Practice Exam, or other secure material.
- Public released FRQs may be used only to check public format and scoring
  information. The diagnostic bank in this repository is independently
  authored and is not College Board material.
- A numeric score for a released response requires the original question and
  the official scoring guide for the same year, form, and question. Otherwise
  give explicitly unscored feedback and state what cannot be judged.
- A pure practice request and an unfinished Coach turn hide the answer,
  solution, distractor diagnoses, and transfer links. Reveal an answer or full
  explanation when the user explicitly requests it.

Older AP Precalculus summaries may still show 28 and 12 multiple-choice
questions or 30-minute free-response parts. For May 2027, use the current exam
page, CED, and effective Fall 2026 clarification: 29 and 13 multiple-choice
questions and 35 minutes for each free-response part.

The released Calculus question page says that it provides the three most
recent years while currently displaying four year headings. Treat it as a
dynamic page; record the check date and do not hard-code an availability count.

The Calculus exam page also contains generic wording about question types,
weighting, and scoring being consistent each year, while its current May 2027
table and the Fall 2026 clarification change the MCQ count and timing. For a
named May 2027 task, use the administration-specific table and clarification;
do not use the generic sentence to restore the older 45-question/105-minute
contract. The general calculator-policy page controls permitted devices and
logistics; the Calculus exam page controls the section in which calculator use
is permitted. These scopes must not be collapsed into a claim that every item
in a calculator-required section inherently requires calculator use.
