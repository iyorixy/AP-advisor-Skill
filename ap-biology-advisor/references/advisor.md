# Advisor Protocol

Read only in Advisor mode. Diagnose the most actionable weakness and prescribe
the smallest useful intervention; do not inventory the course.

## Evidence and diagnosis

Record only what the learner supplied: exam year, deadline, study time,
attempted and correct counts, completion time, written responses, graphs,
calculations, lab reasoning, error log, and confidence. Keep missing values
unknown. Do not assume access to AP Classroom, a scoring guide, raw data, a lab
notebook, or teacher materials. Ask only when the answer would materially change
the intervention; otherwise disclose the proxy.

Classify evidenced problems as one or more of:

- concept/mechanism: a required biological concept, causal chain, scale, or
  structure-function relation is missing or inaccurate;
- model/representation: a diagram, pathway, graph, equation, or level of
  organization is read or constructed incorrectly;
- experimental design: the question, hypothesis, variable, control, replicate,
  procedure, prediction, or inference is mishandled;
- data/statistics: data description, uncertainty, calculation, chi-square
  reasoning, or hypothesis decision is wrong;
- argument/evidence: the claim, evidence selection, reasoning, prediction, or
  connection to a larger biological principle is unsupported;
- reading/completeness: a condition, unit, figure detail, task verb, or required
  response part is missed;
- pacing: a viable method is too slow after other causes are ruled out.

Separate observed symptoms from hypothesized causes. Infer causes only from
work, annotations, or an error log. Accuracy, time, and self-report are
provisional ranking proxies. Never treat Topic order as a dependency graph.

## Minimum intervention and exit

Rank by evidence strength, impact, transfer leverage, deadline, and available
time. Preserve demonstrated strengths with light maintenance. Select one to
three tasks unless the learner asks for broader coverage.

Each task includes:

- **Why now:** evidence, cause hypothesis, uncertainty, and priority reason;
- **Practice:** a concrete method and bounded amount;
- **Exit standard:** measurable accuracy, completeness, evidence use,
  independence, or time threshold.

Map and validate the relevant content Topic and Science Practice; metadata is
not the priority reason. Validate only a Topic actually shown by the learner's
evidence. When only a cross-course Practice is established, use:

    Primary Content Topic: not established
    Primary Science Practice: 5.D

Choose one primary Practice when several are plausible, and list supporting
Practices only when the intervention actually uses them. Retest with an unseen
item that changes organism, context, model, design, data, or evidence. State the
passing result. Until it occurs, call the intervention provisional and do not
claim mastery.

Give only the requested horizon; do not create a calendar unless requested.
